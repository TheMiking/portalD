Ranking (1 = easiest → 15 = hardest)

Feathers / Fur

Very accessible, low skill to collect intact (plucking or skinning fur left on a pelt is different). Main issues are cleanliness and preserving structure.

External scutes / scales (small reptiles, fish scales)

Often readily removable; preservation/arrangement matters but extraction itself is straightforward.

Hide / pelt (intact leather)

Commonly harvested; requires decent technique to skin cleanly and preserve (prevent tears, bacterial spoilage). More difficult on large, thick hides or armored animals.

Muscle / meat (bulk cuts)

Accessible but getting clean, usable cuts without contaminating or damaging underlying tissues takes butchery skill. Preservation (cooling) is important.

Hard claws, hooves, beaks, teeth

Small, dense structures — easy to collect once separated from soft tissue, but may need careful cleaning and drying to avoid cracking.

Cartilage (ears, nose, joint pads)

Moderately easy but deforms or shrinks if not handled/preserved properly.

Bones (large, intact bones for tools or display)

Removing large bones cleanly requires dissection skill and sometimes mechanical effort; marrow removal and cleaning without cracking needs care.

Exoskeleton / carapace (crustaceans, insects, armoured inverts)

Extraction can be easy for small specimens but preserving an intact, uncracked carapace — or removing it from soft tissues without damage — is tricky.

Tendon / ligament (intact lengths for cordage/use)

Strongly anchored and often thread-like; harvesting long, continuous lengths without shredding requires careful dissection and technique.

Organs (liver, heart, kidneys)

Internal location increases contamination risk; some organs are fragile and spoil quickly. Requires speed and sanitary technique to keep them usable.

Marrow (sterile sample from long bones)

Accessing marrow cleanly without contaminating it with bacteria/soil needs proper tools and technique.

Eyes / optic structures

Extremely delicate, easily damaged, and degrade fast — harvesting intact ocular tissue for study or display is difficult.

Nervous tissue / brain / spinal cord

Very fragile, highly susceptible to rapid decay and contamination. Removing intact nervous tissue (especially vertebrate brains) needs precision and rapid preservation.

Vascular system / intact vessels (for grafts or specialized use)

Vessels are thin-walled and collapse; harvesting long, unpunctured lengths free of blood clots or contamination is technically demanding.

Microscale tissues or sterile internal glands (pituitary, adrenal, sterile marrow biopsy-quality samples)

Highest difficulty: very small, deep, contamination-sensitive, and often require surgical tools and lab-grade preservation immediately to be “proper.”
